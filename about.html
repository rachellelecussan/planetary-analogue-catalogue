About the catalogue

The Planetary Analogue Catalogue is an open, searchable map of places on Earth that scientists use to study other worlds: Mars, the Moon, the icy moons and the early Earth. Each entry brings together the science, geology, access and safety information that students, researchers and lecturers need to find a site, judge whether it fits their question and plan a visit.

Every value in an entry comes from a cited source. Entries are reviewed before they are marked as verified, and the full dataset is free to reuse.

How hazard and access ratings work

Ratings run from 1 to 5 against written definitions, so different contributors should give the same site the same number. A hazard rating describes the highest hazard a normal visitor meets, not the worst case anywhere at the site. Each rating shows when it was last reviewed. Ratings are a starting point for your own risk assessment, never a replacement for one.

Sensitive sites

Fragile sites, such as living stromatolites, and sites on Indigenous or community land are shown only approximately, to about 10 km or to the region. Entries name the people and authorities to ask for permission where known. Please follow local rules, take only permitted samples and avoid disturbing living communities.
